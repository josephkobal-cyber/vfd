# How to Greet Walk-In Visitors When Your Front Desk Is Unstaffed

At an unstaffed front desk, a walk-in visitor needs two things: a quick way to reach a real person, and a way to sign in. A Virtual Front Desk station in the lobby gives them both. The visitor taps a button and starts a live video call with an employee or a receptionist, who answers from a computer, a phone or Microsoft Teams, wherever they are. Visitors who only need to sign in do it on screen, and their host is notified automatically.

## Why video works when nobody is behind the desk

A front desk doesn't need someone sitting behind it all day. What visitors want is to see a face and know someone is taking care of them. For most of them, it makes no difference whether that person is standing at the counter or talking to them on a screen, as long as a live human answers.

That's also why a phone answering service doesn't solve this problem. It handles callers. A visitor in your lobby is standing there in person, often with a delivery, an appointment or a question that takes ten seconds to answer if they can just show you. They need to be seen, greeted and pointed to the right person. Video gives them that, and it lets whoever answers see who is actually there.

## How video reception works at an unstaffed front desk

A station is the Virtual Front Desk web app running on a device in your lobby, such as a Neat Frame, an iPad, an Android tablet or a Windows PC. The welcome screen shows the buttons you configure.

1. The visitor taps a button, for example "Reception", "Deliveries" or a department name.
2. The station calls the person or group behind that button, and the visitor sees a message asking them to wait while the call connects.
3. An employee or receptionist answers from the Virtual Front Desk web dashboard, the iOS or Android app, Microsoft Teams, or a video-call link in WhatsApp.
4. The visitor and the person answering talk face to face.

Buttons don't only place calls. A button can also start a sign-in form, show a PDF such as a floor map, display a QR code, or open a web page inside the station, such as a booking form or a page from your website.

## What can the receptionist do during the call?

Almost anything they'd do at the counter. The call window in the dashboard includes:

- **ID capture:** the visitor holds their ID up to the station camera and the receptionist saves the image.
- **Documents for signature:** send a PDF to the station screen and ask the visitor to add their name, email and signature.
- **Screenshots, QR codes and on-screen messages:** take a snapshot, show a link as a QR code, or send a short text to the screen.
- **Hold and screen sharing:** put the visitor on hold, or share your screen to show a map or a document.
- **Live translated captions:** read what the visitor says, translated into your dashboard's language.
- **Enterprise tools:** scan documents with the visitor's own phone, print to a printer beside the station, and take card payments on a Stripe terminal.

Everything captured during a call is saved in the call log. One caveat: these tools live in the Virtual Front Desk dashboard. Calls answered in Microsoft Teams don't include them, which is worth knowing before you decide who answers where.

## Who answers the call?

Your own people. Virtual Front Desk provides the software and the station, not the receptionists. Calls go to an in-house receptionist on another floor or working from home, a group of employees, or a remote reception service you already work with.

When a button calls a group, you choose how it rings:

- **Broadcast:** everyone in the group rings at once, and the first person to answer takes the call.
- **Queue:** members ring one after another, 30 seconds each by default.
- **List:** the station shows the people in the group, and the visitor picks who to call.

If nobody answers, the call forwards to the group's phone number, after 30 seconds by default. A user who is offline or misses a call can have it forwarded to their mobile or landline the same way. The point is simple: a visitor should never be left standing in front of a screen that rings forever.

## Can one receptionist cover several lobbies?

Yes. Because the person answering isn't tied to a desk, every station in every building can call the same receptionist or group. A clinic network, a coworking operator or a company with several offices can run reception from one place.

On the Enterprise plan, live monitoring adds a view of the lobbies themselves. The receptionist sees a low-resolution live feed of up to four stations at a time on the dashboard, notices a visitor waiting and starts a video call in one click. Monitoring feeds have no audio and are never recorded.

## Sign-in without a receptionist

Plenty of visitors don't need a conversation at all. They need to sign in and let their host know they've arrived. They do that on the station by themselves: name, any questions you ask, a selfie or a signed document if you require one. The host is notified automatically by video call, phone call, SMS, email or Microsoft Teams, and on the Enterprise plan a badge prints at the end of the form.

Expected visitors can skip the form entirely with Express sign-in. They fill it in on their phone before arriving and check in at the station with a QR code in a few seconds. Our guide to [QR code visitor sign-in](/blog/qr-code-visitor-sign-in) explains the full flow.

## After hours and quiet times

Every station can have office hours. Outside them, it hides its buttons and shows a closed screen with your message and the next opening time, so visitors aren't left calling an empty building. Each button can also have its own schedule, which lets you show different options in the evening, such as a phone-only button for an on-call manager.

Individual employees can set call hours too, so the station stops ringing them once their shift ends.

## Answering common questions before anyone picks up

Many lobby questions are the same ones over and over: where to park, where the washroom is, which floor a department is on. The Virtual Front Desk AI Agent, a paid add-on available on every plan, greets visitors at the station, answers those questions out loud and starts sign-in for them. When it can't help, it calls reception through the station's buttons. Our article on [what an AI receptionist is](/blog/what-is-an-ai-receptionist) covers how it works and when it hands off.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Virtual Front Desk provide the receptionist?

No. Virtual Front Desk provides the software and the station. Calls go to your own employees, your own receptionist, or a remote reception service you already work with, wherever they happen to be working.

### Can employees answer visitor calls from their phone?

Yes. Employees can answer from the Virtual Front Desk iOS or Android app, from the web dashboard, or in Microsoft Teams. Answering video calls inside Microsoft Teams requires a Teams Phone Standard license, which is a Microsoft requirement.

### What happens if nobody answers the video call?

The call forwards to a phone number, after 30 seconds by default. That can be the group's phone number or the user's own mobile or landline, so a visitor at an unstaffed desk always reaches someone.

### Are video calls recorded?

No. Virtual Front Desk video calls are live only and are never recorded. Live monitoring feeds on the Enterprise plan are not recorded either, and they have no audio.

### Can one receptionist cover several locations?

Yes. Every station can call the same receptionist or group, wherever they work. On the Enterprise plan, live monitoring lets the receptionist watch up to four lobbies at once and start a call from the feed.
