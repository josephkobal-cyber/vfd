# QR Code Visitor Sign-In: How Touchless Check-In Works and What to Look For

QR code visitor sign-in lets an expected visitor fill in the sign-in form on their own phone before they arrive, then check in at the lobby with a single scan. In Virtual Front Desk, this is called Express sign-in. The host invites the visitor from Outlook, Google Calendar or the dashboard, and the visitor completes everything ahead of time. At the station, a QR code or a 4-digit code signs them in, the host is notified, and on the Enterprise plan a visitor badge prints.

## How does QR code sign-in work?

Express sign-in has five steps, and the visitor only spends a few seconds at the station for the last one.

1. **The host invites the visitor.** Any host can send the invitation from the Outlook add-in, the Google Calendar add-on or the Virtual Front Desk dashboard. It goes out by email, SMS or both.
2. **The visitor completes the form on their phone.** The invitation links to your sign-in form. The visitor answers your questions, takes a selfie, photographs their ID and signs any document you require, on their own phone or computer.
3. **The visitor receives their codes.** Each pre-registered visitor gets a QR code and a 4-digit code, and can use either one on arrival.
4. **The visitor checks in at the station.** In the lobby, they tap "I have a QR code" and hold their phone up to the station camera, or they type the 4-digit code.
5. **Everything after the scan is automatic.** The visitor is signed in, the host is notified, and a badge prints if your site uses them.

The record that lands in your sign-in logs is the same one you'd get from a sign-in at the station, down to the badge, which shows up to 3 fields from the form. You just collected it earlier.

## What does "touchless" mean in practice?

Touchless means the shared screen stops being the place where visitors type. With Express sign-in, every field, photo and signature happens on the visitor's own device, in their own time, before they reach your building. At the station, what's left is one tap on "I have a QR code" and the scan. Nobody waits behind a contractor who is spelling out their company name on a tablet.

It's also faster for your hosts. A visitor who pre-registered shows up already expected. The host gets the arrival notice the moment the code is scanned, so there's no "who are you here to see?" conversation at the desk.

## Hosts invite their own visitors from their calendar

Pre-registration doesn't need a receptionist in the middle. Hosts invite visitors from the tools they already have open.

- **Outlook:** with the [Virtual Front Desk for Outlook add-in](https://help.virtualfrontdesk.com/en/article/outlook-calendar-add-in-for-pre-registration-or-express-qr-code-sign-in), a host creates the meeting, opens the add-in, picks the location, host and sign-in form, and clicks Send Pre-Registrations. External attendees are selected automatically. An administrator installs the add-in once from the Microsoft 365 admin center. It works in the Outlook calendar, not in the Teams calendar.
- **Google Calendar:** the [Virtual Front Desk add-on for Google Calendar](https://help.virtualfrontdesk.com/en/article/google-calendar-add-on-for-pre-registration-and-express-qr-code-sign-in) works the same way from a saved event in Google Calendar on the web. The event organizer sends the pre-registrations.
- **Dashboard:** create an invitation for one visitor, or use Bulk invitations when a group is coming in, such as a training day or a client delegation.

A manager booking an interview sends the invitation in the same step as the meeting invite. The candidate arrives with the form done.

## Single-use codes, with a fallback that never blocks a visit

Every QR code is single-use. Once a visitor signs in with it, the code stops working, so it can't be forwarded to someone else and reused. A visitor coming back on another day gets a new invitation.

If a visitor loses the invitation or their phone battery dies, nothing breaks. They sign in at the station like any walk-in, and the host is notified the same way. That fallback matters more than it sounds: a sign-in system that turns a dead phone into a stuck visitor creates more work at the desk, not less.

## Walk-ins and pre-registered visitors on the same station

QR codes are only for pre-registered visitors. Walk-in visitors use the regular sign-in form on the station screen. Both run on the same station, so a lobby handles expected guests and unannounced visitors without separate hardware or a second queue.

Here's how the two compare:

- **Who uses it:** walk-in sign-in is for anyone who arrives unannounced. Express sign-in is for visitors a host invited ahead of time.
- **Where the form is filled in:** walk-ins fill it in on the station screen. Pre-registered visitors fill it in on their own phone or computer before the visit.
- **What happens at the station:** a walk-in completes every field and photo there. A pre-registered visitor scans the QR code or types the 4-digit code.
- **Host notification:** automatic in both cases.
- **Visitor badge (Enterprise):** prints at the end of the form for walk-ins, and right after the scan for pre-registered visitors.
- **Record in your sign-in logs:** both are saved and kept 12 months by default.

## Keeping track of who's expected

Every invitation has a status in the form's Pre-registration tab:

- **Pending:** the invitation was sent, but the visitor hasn't filled in the form yet.
- **Pre-Registered:** the visitor completed the form before arriving.
- **Completed:** the visitor arrived and checked in at the station.

If a visitor is still Pending the day before, you can resend the invite with one click. The pre-registration logs in History also show invitations sent, arrivals, no-shows and visits still scheduled, which gives front-desk and security teams a simple view of the day ahead.

## When is pre-registration worth turning on?

If your lobby sees the same contractors, clients and candidates every week, turn it on. The more regular visitors you have, the more time Express sign-in saves, both at the station and for the hosts waiting on them. Recurring meetings, interviews, maintenance contractors and client visits are the obvious wins.

If your site gets a handful of unplanned visitors a week, the regular station sign-in is usually enough. You can still turn pre-registration on later for the visits you do plan.

## What to look for in a QR code visitor sign-in system

When you compare tools, these questions separate a QR code that just opens a web form from a sign-in system that actually saves time:

- Can the visitor complete the whole form on their phone, including photos, questions and signatures, before arriving?
- Can hosts send invitations from Outlook or Google Calendar without logging in to the visitor management dashboard?
- Are the QR codes single-use?
- What happens when a visitor loses their code?
- Does the scan notify the host and print the badge automatically?
- Can walk-in and pre-registered visitors use the same station?
- Can you see who has pre-registered, who arrived and who didn't show?
- Is pre-registration included in the base plan, or sold as an add-on?

## Frequently asked questions

### Can walk-in visitors sign in with a QR code?

No. In Virtual Front Desk, QR codes are issued only to pre-registered visitors through Express sign-in. Walk-in visitors sign in on the station screen with the regular sign-in form, and their host is notified automatically in the same way.

### Can a QR code be used more than once?

No. Each Express sign-in QR code is single-use. After the visitor signs in, the code stops working, which prevents it from being shared or reused. A visitor coming back on another day receives a new invitation.

### What happens if a visitor loses their QR code?

The visitor signs in at the station the same way a walk-in does, by filling in the sign-in form on screen. The host is still notified automatically, so a lost code never stops a visit.

### Which Virtual Front Desk plans include Express sign-in?

Express sign-in is available on every Virtual Front Desk plan, along with the Outlook add-in and the Google Calendar add-on. Badge printing at the end of sign-in requires the Enterprise plan, and video call notifications to the host start on the Professional plan.

### Can hosts pre-register visitors from Google Calendar?

Yes. Hosts can pre-register visitors from the Google Calendar add-on, the Outlook add-in or the Virtual Front Desk dashboard. With the Google Calendar add-on, the event organizer sends the pre-registrations from a saved event.
