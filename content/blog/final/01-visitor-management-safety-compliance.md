# Visitor Management for Safety and Compliance: How to Know Who Is Onsite

Visitor management for safety and compliance comes down to one question: can you say who is in your building right now, and prove who was there on any given day? Virtual Front Desk answers it by replacing the paper logbook with a sign-in station in the lobby. Visitors sign in, sign out and sign any required documents on screen. Every record lands in the cloud, the host hears about the arrival automatically, and you can text every visitor still onsite in an emergency.

## Why the paper logbook stops being enough

The visitor log isn't new. Security desks have kept one for decades, and the reasons haven't changed: you need to know who came in, who they came to see, and whether they agreed to your site rules. What has changed is how you keep it.

A paper logbook does the job on a quiet Tuesday. It struggles everywhere else. Handwriting is hard to read. Every visitor can see the names of the people who signed in before them. Finding a specific visit from four months ago means flipping through binders. And very few people remember to write down the time they left, so the book tells you who arrived, not who is still inside.

There's a staffing cost too. The guard who keeps the log is usually the same person who phones the host and waits for someone to come down. That's time spent on the phone instead of watching the lobby.

## What a compliance-ready visitor record should capture

Before you compare tools, decide what a complete record looks like for your site. For most organizations, it covers six things.

- **Who the visitor is.** You need it to identify people during and after the visit. Virtual Front Desk asks for the name (the only mandatory field) plus any fields you add, such as company.
- **Who they came to see.** Every visitor should be someone's responsibility. The visitor picks their host, or the form contacts one host directly.
- **When they arrived and left.** This is what gives you an accurate count of who is onsite. Each sign-in is time-stamped, and an optional sign-out button records the departure.
- **Proof of identity.** Higher-security sites need verification. The station can take a selfie and a photo of the front and back of an ID card.
- **What they agreed to.** NDAs and safety briefings need evidence. Visitors sign PDFs on the station, and each signed copy is stored with the visit.
- **Where they signed in.** Reporting across buildings depends on it. Each station belongs to a location.

## How visitor sign-in works at a Virtual Front Desk station

A station is the Virtual Front Desk web app running in the browser of a device in your lobby, usually a Neat Frame, an iPad, an Android tablet or a Windows PC. The visitor enters the same details they'd write in the logbook, and everything after that is automatic.

1. The visitor taps the sign-in button on the welcome screen.
2. They enter their name and any other fields you ask for, such as company or reason for visit.
3. They take a selfie with the station camera, if you require one.
4. They read and sign any required document, such as an NDA or your site safety rules.
5. They hold the front, then the back of their ID card up to the camera, if your site requires it.
6. The station notifies the host and, if you use badges, prints a visitor badge.

The native form keeps things simple: text fields, required or optional. If you need dropdowns or conditional questions, you can embed an online form from Microsoft Forms, Google Forms or Jotform inside the sign-in flow.

## Who gets notified when a visitor signs in?

The person the visitor came to see. Depending on each employee's settings and your plan, the notification arrives as a video call, a phone call, an SMS or an email. On the Essential plan, hosts are notified by SMS and email. Video and phone calls start on the Professional plan, and employees can take those calls in the Virtual Front Desk app or [directly in Microsoft Teams](https://help.virtualfrontdesk.com/en/article/ms-teams-integration-for-video-calls-call-queue-auto-attendant).

On the Enterprise plan, the same step can print a visitor badge. You choose what goes on it: a header such as "Visitor", up to three fields from the form, your logo, the visitor's selfie, and the date and time.

The practical effect is that nobody at the desk has to pick up the phone. Your guard stops being a switchboard and gets back to the job they were hired for: walking the lobby, watching the doors and helping people who need it.

## How do you know who is in the building right now?

Turn on sign-out. Without it, any digital log is still just a list of arrivals.

With sign-out enabled on the form, you add a Sign out button to the station. Leaving visitors tap it, type the first letters of their name and pick themselves from the list. The sign-in logs in your dashboard then show who signed in today and hasn't signed out yet. If someone forgets, an admin can sign them out from their row.

That list is what you need when the alarm goes off. From the sign-in logs, owners, admins and managers can send an [emergency message](https://help.virtualfrontdesk.com/en/article/emergency-message): an SMS to every visitor who signed in today, hasn't signed out and gave a mobile number. You choose which locations receive it, and you can save templates for a fire evacuation, a lockdown or a building closure so the right instructions go out in seconds. Two settings make this work: the form needs a mobile number field (make it required), and the Emergency message option must be turned on for that form.

## Express sign-in for expected visitors

For visitors you know are coming, the fastest sign-in is the one they finish before they arrive. Hosts pre-register visitors from the dashboard, from Outlook or from Google Calendar, and the visitor completes the form on their own phone, selfie and ID photos included. At the station, they scan a QR code or type a 4-digit code and they're in. The [help center guide to Express sign-in](https://help.virtualfrontdesk.com/en/article/visitor-sign-form-and-sign-out-option) walks through the full flow.

## Keeping, finding and exporting visitor records

Every sign-in lands in History, not in a binder. Authorized users can search and filter the logs by date, location and station, open a visit to see the visitor's answers, selfie and signed documents, and download the record. Export All downloads the whole log when you need it for an audit or a report.

Sign-in records are kept for 12 months by default and then deleted automatically. If your policy calls for a different retention period, it can be changed.

Access follows roles. Owners, admins, managers and users each see and do only what their role allows, and people only see the locations they've been given access to. For a company with several buildings, that means one dashboard with a consistent record at every site, without everyone seeing everything.

## What to look for in a visitor management tool for safety and compliance

If you're comparing options, these are the questions that separate a digital guest book from a tool you can rely on in an audit or an evacuation:

- **Sign-out:** can visitors sign out, and can an admin close a forgotten visit?
- **A live onsite list:** can you see who is in the building now, per location?
- **Emergency messaging:** can you reach every visitor still onsite from one screen?
- **Signed documents:** are NDAs and safety acknowledgements stored with the visit record?
- **Identity:** can you capture a selfie and an ID photo when a site requires it?
- **Retention and export:** how long are records kept, and can you change it and export them?
- **Host notification:** does the host hear about the arrival without anyone at the desk making a call?
- **Security:** where is the data hosted, how is it encrypted, and what has been independently audited?

## Frequently asked questions

### What hardware does a Virtual Front Desk station run on?

A station runs in the browser of a recent iPad, Android tablet, Windows PC, Neat Frame or Neat Board. There's nothing to install on the device, and a station can be live in minutes. Virtual Front Desk recommends the Neat Frame for most lobbies because it locks into kiosk mode automatically and can be managed remotely.

### How do I know which visitors are still onsite?

Turn on sign-out in your visitor sign-in form and add a Sign out button to the station. The sign-in logs then show every visitor who signed in today and hasn't signed out. Admins can sign out a visitor who forgot, which keeps the list accurate for an emergency.

### Can I send an emergency alert to visitors in the building?

Yes. Owners, admins and managers can send an SMS from the sign-in logs to every visitor who signed in today, hasn't signed out and gave a mobile number. The sign-in form needs a mobile number field and the Emergency message option turned on.

### How long are visitor records kept?

Virtual Front Desk keeps sign-in records for 12 months by default and then deletes them automatically. The retention period can be changed if your policy requires it, and records can be exported at any time.

### Does a digital sign-in replace the security guard?

No. It takes over the paperwork and the phone calls, not the guard's role. With the log and host notifications automated, the guard has more time to watch the lobby, help visitors and respond when something looks wrong.
